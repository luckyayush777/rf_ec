# Godot behavior contracts

Moved from the root navigation guide on 2026-10-03. Read the contracts relevant
to the owner and its callers before editing. Search this file by feature or symbol;
keep cross-controller requirements together. Paths are repository-relative.

## Contracts to preserve

- Rules derive service dependencies from metadata, including dynamic screw lists and cooler-before-fan refit order. Keep decision logic in the rule evaluator and animation/placement guards in controllers.
- T opens the tool bag from anywhere (`workbench.open_tool_menu(true)` skips the reach check); in a service close-up it records `focus_return` and `close_tool_menu` reopens that part's close-up after the pick. Clicking the roll still needs reach. `tests/tool_hotkey_flow.gd` covers it.
- Cable handling and assembly detachment/refitting require empty hands; the rule action `inspect` permits holding an already-detached assembly with a tool equipped. Placing held parts retains the equipped tool, and holding a detached part does not block screw work on the board; only the screwdriver turns screws. Preserve one equipped tool at a time and busy-action guards.
- The Godot GPU root uses 0.25 scale. Inspection, detached-part rotation, screw tray transfers and testing must preserve global basis scale; orthonormalizing alone or replacing a basis with a unit rotation enlarges the card. Runtime holder resizing and testing-desk alignment belong to `workbench.gd`; the monitor remains independent of desk scaling.
- Held GPU/fan/heatsink sit on the left, leaving the centre aiming ray clear. A small green/red bench ring uses the actual placement clearance check; GPU and detached assemblies can be placed on the repair desk, and Q returns the carried GPU to its original holder. Placement checks use the authored tabletop world bounds.
- Refitting restores original parent/local transforms. Dust stays attached to its own mesh through removal, placement, storage and refitting. Residue meshes must remain separate from fixed-detail batching.
- Keep the face/tile mapping in `gpu_cleaning.gd`, `dust_overlay.gdshader` and `dust_highlight.gdshader` identical; the debug highlight shares each surface's live mask texture.
- Imported GPU triangle winding is opposite the rendered vertex and picker normals. `gpu_cleaning.gd` flips the cross product before assigning mask faces; changing that convention can leave fan dust counted on the wrong side. Cleaning rays skip the decorative `fan-brand-label` and `hub-center` meshes so the dustable `fan-hub-cap` remains reachable.
- Fan dust generation excludes inward/underside faces (`outward.y < 0.6`); those surfaces can be enclosed by the mounted fan and would leave uncleanable dust. `smoke.gd` checks that generated fan masks can be cleared from visible mounted-fan triangles across randomized runs.
- Godot dust stays with each selected mesh through assembly movement. Mask bytes are thickness (`FILM` to `FELT`): about 85% (`DUST_COVERAGE`) of each cleanable mesh starts dusty, chosen mostly by clump noise, and `deposit_weight` (computed once, with the card in its holder) thickens the fin inlet under the blade sweep, blades toward the hub, upward faces and the board just outside the cooler footprint. Texels with any dust retain a visible opacity floor until erased; filtered fringes beyond them feather out. Thick dust is removed faster per frame than thin film. The debug-only `highlight dust` button toggles a through-part magenta overlay; it activates automatically 30 seconds after a Dev blower pickup if dust remains. Both blowers remove dust only when the nozzle points at a visible part. The Dev blower cleans a wide spot on the aimed surface and loops the supplied air recording. The air blower cleans an `AIR_RADIUS` jet on the aimed surface plus `AIR_SPLASH` on faces turned toward the jet within `AIR_DEPTH` (limited to the focus subject), runs its motor loop (synthesized unless `assets/sounds/air_blower.wav` exists) with wind-up and coast-down even on a clean card, and emits `fan_blown` while aimed at the rotor. Cleaned dust emits puffs/flakes from `dust_puffs.gd`. Each part's remainder clears at 98% with one jingle per part. Job completion and pad scraping are not implemented.
- Godot testing requires an assembled GPU with its fan cable connected and an empty tool hand; a held GPU can be carried directly onto the board. `testing_station.gd` moves the same card to the imported board's PCIe slot and restores its original holder transform on removal. The monitor's deterministic racing display takes its simulated presentation FPS from the core clock reported by `gpu_thermal.gd` (not directly from cleanliness), with uneven frame pacing and hitches while throttling, and draws memory-error artifacts from the VRAM error rate; each presented frame advances the race by the elapsed time, so low rates read as jumps. The game's sound plays only while the monitor is on and connected, from an `AudioStreamGenerator` on the `RacingGame` channel: `advance_demo` submits its speed/bend parameters only on presented frames, `is_stalled()` (a frame more than `STALL_INTERVALS` nominal intervals and `STALL_MIN` late) replays the last `STUTTER_BLOCK` of samples until the next frame, and memory errors add crackling samples. `render_audio()` is deterministic per `reset_simulation()` so tests can check it without an audio device. Keep BENCH's actual rendering responsive. `assets/sounds/gpu_sounds/gpu_attach_short.wav` is the supplied short attachment recording and plays once when the card finishes seating.
- Jobs: `workbench.job_flow` is true when the workbench is the running scene (product play). Scripts that instantiate it (every existing test) get the three-fault walk-in card and the thermal camera on its stand, unchanged; set `bench.job_flow = true` before `add_child` to test the loop. There is one GPU node: without a card it is hidden, and inspection, test-board attachment, debug shortcuts and picker targets refuse it; `repair_jobs.apply_faults` resets it per job. Faults are rolled when an offer is posted; `fault_odds(n)` uses the fixed `FAULT_RATIO`, so P(3 of 3) = 10% and adding a type makes many-fault cards rarer. A new fault needs a `FAULTS` entry (pay, symptoms, persists), `apply_faults` and `problems` cases and a `repair_status` row. Every job has a `source`: techs pay `pay` (their diagnosed faults' prices) and never rate; customers pay `bill(job).dollars` and are rated by `rate`. Service controllers report used material only through a `consumed(item, quantity)` signal with a `bill_of_materials.gd` `CATALOG` id; `repair_jobs.record_use` charges it to the card on the bench and ignores it otherwise. `return_card` pays only when `problems()` is empty (dust complete, paste seated, not dried and at least `GOOD_CONTACT`, bearing not dry and closed), whatever was rolled. The portal is clicked in the world: page controls stay focus-free, page rebuilds are deferred, and mouse events reach the page only through `shop_computer.page_point`. Locked tools stay hidden and cannot be equipped.
- Edge connector: `gpu_connector.gd` owns the damage and the link; `test_monitor.gd` only displays `set_link` (0 = down) and caps its rate with `LINK_FPS`, so a narrow link reads as slow but cool at full clock, distinct from paste and dust. Damage only alters the imported `gold-contact-*` meshes (restored on `set_state("ok")`) plus runtime stub/scar meshes under `edge-connector`, so it travels with the card; the focus view copies `material_override` every frame so tarnish shows live. Jobs must not roll connector faults until a repair exists; `repair_jobs.apply_faults` resets it to `ok` and `problems()` reports it (`UNROLLED` feedback). On the test board, clicking the seated card grabs it for rocking (mouse look is suspended, walking disabled) and E removes it; `testing_station.gd` rewrites the seated transform from `seated_pose` every frame while installed. The loupe is a `VIEW_TOOLS` focus tool: in focus it shows the lens, frames the connector on the full card, clicks call `magnify_at` and the wheel may zoom to `LOUPE_ZOOM`; other tools stay at `VIEW_ZOOM`.
- Each Godot screw has a native recess marker anchored to its original parent/transform in `gpu_service.gd`. The marker remains when the screw moves to the tray; `interaction_picker.gd` exposes its empty center as `screw_hole` only from the visible side, and a rule-checked click immediately seats the associated screw. Holding then tightens it locally; release retains progress. E on the GPU or an already-detached assembly picks it up even with a tool equipped. Focus roots, visible meshes, screw targets and dust progress come from the selected part, including future metadata-defined assemblies. A screwdriver/blower click on the GPU or R while carrying any part opens bench_closeup.gd (E also opens it when not aiming at the mat); its pointer supports screw holds and visible-surface blower cleaning, and its return-tool region keeps focus open; with empty tool hand, focus clicks can unplug the cable or lift rule-approved loosened assemblies; its isolated camera renders live proxies without changing physical transforms.
- The debug disassembly shortcut in `gpu_service.gd` normalizes partial service into a refittable state: cable unplugged, eight screws in tray slots, heatsink then fan laid out upright on the mat just left of the card. Tray slots (`TRAY_*` in `gpu_service.gd`) are in RepairDesk space and screws rest on the tray floor, so moving the desk moves them; the mat layout is computed from the card and mat bounds. `placement_flow.gd` checks both. The debug reassembly shortcut (`gpu_service.debug_reassemble`, via `workbench.debug_reassemble_gpu`) is its inverse from any partial state: it closes an opened fan bearing first, seats the heatsink then the fan (emitting `assembly_seated` so paste and bearing react), tightens every screw home and plugs the cable in; `smoke.gd` checks it. The debug clean shortcut updates every dust mask and completion flag; `workbench.gd` refreshes the connected monitor's simulated rate.
- `testing_station.gd` spins only `fan-rotor` about imported local Y and moves its sibling `fan-brand-label` around the same pivot without reparenting. Dust drives eased visual speed and quiet/loud audio blending while seated, independently of monitor power. Removal stops audio and restores authored rotor/label transforms once after spin-down; a stopped fan's transforms are left alone so `gpu_bearing.gd` can peel and pull them. `blow_fan()` (from `gpu_cleaning.fan_blown`) freewheels an unpowered, closed fan to `AIR_FAN_SPEED`, after which it coasts down at `AIR_COAST`; `gpu_bearing.operate` refuses while `fan_speed > 0`. A dry bearing adds the grind loop while the fan spins, powered or air-blown. The global sound toggle must keep all loops muted across updates.
- Product play is first-person only. `CameraRig.legacy_test_mode` is enabled only by old smoke/service fixtures; no room-orbit controls are exposed in play; the service close-up has its own rotate/zoom camera. Room/furniture collision excludes movable GPU/tools. Proximity and busy guards apply to centred actions; Tab/Escape/focus loss release capture and cancel holds.
- `gpu_thermal.gd` models two heat faults. Dust raises core, VRAM and heatsink; dried die paste (`gpu_paste.gd` contact quality) raises only the core and slightly lowers the heatsink, so a large core-to-heatsink gap is the paste signature. The core is capped at `CORE_LIMIT`; `core_clock()` drops boost bins from `THROTTLE_START` to `THROTTLED_CLOCK` at the limit, and `memory_error_rate()` rises from `MEMORY_ERROR_START` to `MEMORY_ERROR_FULL`. Hot VRAM corrupts the feed but never lowers the clock, so paste faults read as throttling without artifacts. Powered parts settle within about 6 s (`WARMUP_TIME_CONSTANT` 2 s); unpowered cooling stays slow. The die is covered by `heatsink-base` when assembled, so its temperature is read from the test monitor's GPU sensor line, fed by `gpu_thermal.gd`; paste quality is not shown in normal play.
- Paste lives in two runtime layers built by `gpu_paste.gd`: `paste-die` under `gpu-die` and `paste-heatsink` under `heatsink-base`, so each travels with its part. Die cell k faces heatsink cell `mirror(k)` because the heatsink layer is flipped to face outward. Old compound is a per-cell stack: `glaze` (hard top), `crust` (chalky body), `gum` (pasty base), then `film`. `reset_dried` generates both faces in die-cell coordinates (seeded noise): pump-out ridge toward the die edges, drier rim (more glaze) and pastier middle (more gum), fissures, and a torn split between die and heatsink. Keep the packing identical in `gpu_paste.gd` `refresh()` and `paste_layer.gdshader`: `paste_map` RGBA = crust, film, fresh paste thickness, glaze, and `gum_map` R = gum, with dried layers divided by `STACK_DISPLAY`; the shader displaces the layer by the stack and fresh paste and derives smooth relief normals from the same maps. `service_rules.check_surface` owns reach (`cooler-assembly` detached) and tool choice (spudger scrapes, IPA wipe lifts film only where glaze and crust are gone, syringe applies to the die only); the frozen fixtures cover only `check`. The spudger works top-down per cell: glaze (`CHIP_BOOST` faster beside a gap, emitting `chipped`), then crust, then gum, of which `SMEAR` is pushed ahead of the stroke (outward when held still) and lost off the face edge. It also removes fresh paste but leaves film; the IPA wipe also dissolves exposed gum. Each face auto-clears at 5% residue with one jingle. `gpu_service.gd` emits `assembly_seated`/`assembly_detached`; seating the cooler presses paste (cells over the die cap at the bond line, excess flows onto the package margin) and sets contact quality; lifting splits the pressed paste between faces and reports the imprint. Refitting over bad or missing paste is allowed; the thermal model shows the consequence. A ray that slips through the subdivided layer onto the die or heatsink base counts as that face. Test-board attachment powers the card even if the monitor is off; detaching cools it gradually. Internal fan/cooler dust dominates memory heat. Compare repairs under the same powered load. The physical thermal LCD and enlarged viewer both show the temperature and identity of the visible surface at the crosshair. The thermal viewer uses a separate world of visible mesh proxies (no through-part rendering), fixed 20-100 degrees C palette and approximate emissivity; it never replaces the live dust materials.
- `gpu_bearing.gd` owns fan-bearing state. `service_rules.check_opening` orders the openings (`hub-label` before `fan-rotor`, closing in reverse) and allows bare hands or the spudger/IPA wipe/fan oiler; `check_surface` gates the `shaft` (IPA wipe) and `bearing` (oiler) behind the detached fan and pulled rotor via `needsOpen`. Refitting the rotor presses the sticker back on and sets `dry = not (shaft_clean and oil_drops > 0)`. `gpu_service.refit_block` stops the fan mounting with its rotor out. The supplied `assets/sounds/gpu_sounds/sticker_peel_gpu_use.wav` plays with +15 dB gain (the recording is quiet) and sets the peel duration. An optional `assets/sounds/fan_grind.wav` replaces the synthesized placeholder grind in `testing_station.gd`.
- Every AudioStreamPlayer sets `bus` to its `audio_mix.gd` channel; add new sounds to `CHANNELS` and route them, or `audio_mix_flow.gd` fails. Per-player `volume_db` stays as a fixed trim or code-driven blend (fan loops, grind, the M-key mute); the mixer scales buses on top. Levels changed in play save into `res://default_bus_layout.tres` when run from the editor (the committed project mix) and to `user://audio_mix.cfg` in exported builds. Tests must stop `hud.audio_save_timer` rather than save. After freeing the bench, a test waits about 10 process frames before `quit()` if any sound may still be playing (for example the 0.6 s set-down after placing a card or assembly): the audio server drops a playing sound's playback a few frames after its player is freed, and quitting sooner reports the stream as leaked at exit, which fails the runner.
- The monitor screen in `scenes/test_monitor.tscn` uses a PlaneMesh so its texture spans the entire face. Godot's BoxMesh UV atlas would crop the racing image to one portion of the screen.
